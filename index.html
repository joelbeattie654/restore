Exhibition Manifesto

Adaptive reuse is architectural storytelling. By splicing historical engineering blueprints, structural steel interventions, and modern programmatic needs, these works interrogate how our built heritage houses contemporary life without erasing historical memory.

Project 01

Titanic Hotel Belfast

Former Harland & Wolff Drawing Offices & Headquarters

D/N
← Drag slider to compare Day & Night states → Square Format Archive

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: Built in phases between 1889 and 1911 as the administrative and drafting epicenter for Harland & Wolff.
  • Original Architects: Designed by W.H. Pim, with subsequent grand expansions directed by James Logan and leading shipyard engineers.
  • Historical Use: The world-renowned drawing offices where the structural plans for RMS Titanic, Olympic, and Britannic were drafted, alongside the executive headquarters commanding global shipbuilding supremacy.

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Lead Conservation Architects: Consarc Conservation & RMI Architects.
  • Structural Engineers: Albert Fry Associates.
  • Intervention Strategy: Meticulous preservation methodology allowing sensitive structural insertions to transform soaring three-storey barrel-vaulted drafting halls and executive offices into a luxury 119-room hotel, maintaining historic tilework and Belfast brickwork intact.

03. Cinematic Architecture

Similar grand industrial-to-hospitality atmosphere found in films like The Great Gatsby (industrial ambition meeting luxury) and Titanic (historical grandeur).

Project 02

Mersey Street Primary School

Grade B1 Listed School Transformed into Social Housing, East Belfast

D/N
← Drag slider to compare Day & Night states → Square Format Archive

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: Designed and constructed prior to the Second World War.
  • Original Architect: Reginald Sherman Wilshire (Appointed architect to the Belfast Education Committee in 1926).
  • Historical Use: Part of an esteemed 'family' of 26 exceptional schools designed by Wilshire across Belfast, serving as a pillar of educational and community life for working-class families in East Belfast (Grade B1 Listed).

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Client / Developer: Connswater Homes.
  • Intervention Strategy: Transformed into 30 high-quality social housing apartments (28 two-bed and 2 one-bed units). Utilized advanced cathodic protection (DuoGuard hybrid anode systems) to repair reinforced concrete degradation without altering historic masonry facades, preserving original classroom windows and structural columns while carving out multi-level residential layouts.

03. Cinematic Architecture

Shares the institutional brick aesthetic and community rebirth seen in To Kill a Mockingbird or The Shawshank Redemption (historic brick institutions repurposed).

Project 03

The WeavingWorks Belfast

Historic Linen Warehouse, Alfred Street / Linen Quarter

D/N
← Drag slider to compare Day & Night states → Square Format Archive

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: Erected between 1907 and 1908.
  • Original Architect: William J. Roome.
  • Historical Use: Built as an imposing four-storey red brick linen warehouse for Greeves, Ridgeway & Co., prime linen and damask manufacturers during Belfast's zenith as "Linenopolis"—the global capital of the linen trade.

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Lead Architectural Firm: RMI Architects (Rejen Group).
  • Intervention Strategy: Conversion of a derelict, deep-plan industrial warehouse into vibrant mixed-use spaces and modern creative offices. Preserved heavy timber internal floor joists, cast-iron columns, and external red brick masonry façades while introducing modern vertical circulation, thermal insulation envelopes, and high-performance glazing.

03. Cinematic Architecture

Evokes the industrial loft aesthetic found in The Social Network or Blade Runner (early industrial spaces turned into modern digital tech hubs).

Project 04

The Savoy Bangor

Former Savoy Hotel & Entertainment Venue, County Down

D/N
← Drag slider to compare Day & Night states → Square Format Archive

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: Constructed in 1932 for Mr J. Gaston (Northern Ireland Tours); extended and refaced in 1933.
  • Original Architect: John McBride Neill (renowned Ulster cinema and hospitality architect).
  • Historical Use: A quintessential interwar seaside entertainment and hotel landmark anchoring Bangor's golden age as a bustling coastal holiday resort for Belfast day-trippers.

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Lead Architectural Firm: McGarry-Moon Architects (Coleraine).
  • Intervention Strategy: Award-winning redevelopment transforming two post-war, derelict hotel structures into contemporary residential apartments. The architects balanced coastal conservation constraints with modern spatial planning, introducing dynamic lightwells, crisp contemporary metal cladding accents, and high-density seaside living units.

03. Cinematic Architecture

Shares the whimsical coastal optimism and retro-futuristic styling of Meet the Robinsons or Wes Anderson's The Grand Budapest Hotel (historic resort architecture).
