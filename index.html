Exhibition Manifesto

Adaptive reuse is not merely conservation; it is architectural collage. By splicing original engineering blueprints, structural steel insertions, and contemporary programmatic needs, these four works interrogate how Northern Ireland's built heritage can house modern living, tourism, and community without erasing historical trauma or industrial memory.

Project 01

Titanic Hotel Belfast

Former Harland & Wolff Drawing Offices & Headquarters

Fig 1.1: Isometric View / Artistic Intervention

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: 1889–1911 (Built in phases as the administrative hub for Harland & Wolff).
  • Original Architect: W.H. Pim and subsequent expansions by James Logan and other shipyard engineers.
  • Historical Use: The world-famous drawing offices where RMS Titanic, Olympic, and Britannic were designed, alongside the main corporate headquarters directing global shipbuilding supremacy.

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Lead Conservation Architects: Consarc Conservation & RMI Architects.
  • Structural Engineers: Albert Fry Associates.
  • Intervention Strategy: Careful planning and methodology enabled sensitive structural insertions to convert the high-ceilinged drafting rooms and executive offices into a luxury 119-room hotel. Preserved the iconic three-storey high barrel-vaulted ceilings, original tilework, and Belfast brickwork without compromising historic listing status.

03. Artwork & Concept

This digital collage stitches together archival blueprints from the 1908 drafting expansion with contemporary thermal mapping. It explores the tension between dead labor (the blueprints of lost vessels) and living capital (luxury hospitality tourism), capturing how heavy industrial architecture transforms into sanitized heritage leisure spaces.

Project 02

The WeavingWorks Belfast

Historic Linen Warehouse, Alfred Street / Linen Quarter

Fig 2.1: Facade Elevation & Urban Context

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: 1907–1908.
  • Original Architect: William J. Roome.
  • Historical Use: Built as a grand four-storey red brick linen warehouse for Greeves, Ridgeway & Co., linen and damask manufacturers during Belfast's height as "Linenopolis"—the global epicentre of the linen trade.

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Lead Architectural Firm: RMI Architects (Rejen Group).
  • Intervention Strategy: Conversion of a derelict, deep-plan industrial warehouse into vibrant mixed-use spaces and modern creative offices. Preserved heavy timber internal floor joists, cast-iron columns, and external red brick masonry façades while introducing modern vertical circulation, thermal insulation envelopes, and high-performance glazing.

03. Artwork & Concept

The artwork superimposes 1907 mercantile ledger entries over the exposed structural bays of Alfred Street. It acts as an inquiry into how post-industrial urban cores can be reinhabited by knowledge-economy workspaces without severing the physical weight of Victorian trade infrastructure.

Project 03

The Savoy Bangor

Former Savoy Hotel & Entertainment Venue, County Down

Fig 3.1: Coastal Elevation & Re-imagined Massing

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: Built in 1932 for Mr J. Gaston (Northern Ireland Tours); extended and refaced in 1933.
  • Original Architect: John McBride Neill (noted Ulster cinema and hospitality architect).
  • Historical Use: A quintessential interwar seaside entertainment and hotel destination anchoring Bangor's golden age as a bustling coastal holiday resort for Belfast day-trippers.

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Lead Architectural Firm: McGarry-Moon Architects (Coleraine).
  • Intervention Strategy: Award-winning redevelopment transforming two post-war, derelict hotel structures into contemporary residential apartments. The architects balanced coastal conservation constraints with modern spatial planning, introducing dynamic lightwells, crisp contemporary metal cladding accents, and high-density seaside living units.

03. Artwork & Concept

This piece collages John McBride Neill's original 1933 art-deco curved facade blueprints with recent demolition photography. It interrogates the shift in seaside towns from seasonal leisure economies to permanent commuter residential settlements.

Project 04

Mersey Street Primary School

Grade B1 Listed School Transformed into Social Housing, East Belfast

Fig 4.1: Fenestration & Civic Scale Analysis

01. Chronology & Origin

  • Construction Dates: Designed prior to World War II.
  • Original Architect: Reginald Sherman Wilshire (Appointed architect to the Belfast Education Committee in 1926).
  • Historical Use: One of a "family" of 26 exceptional schools designed by Wilshire across Belfast, serving as an institutional educational hub for generations of working-class children in East Belfast. Grade B1 Listed building.

02. Adaptive Reuse & Architects

  • Client / Developer: Connswater Homes.
  • Intervention Strategy: Conversion into 30 high-quality social housing apartments (28 two-bed and 2 one-bed units). Utilized advanced cathodic protection (DuoGuard hybrid anode systems) to treat reinforced concrete distress without altering historic facades, preserving original classroom windows and structural columns while carving habitable multi-level residential layouts within the soaring classroom volumes.

03. Artwork & Concept

This collage juxtaposes Wilshire's symmetrical 1920s educational blueprint elevations with modern structural anode diagrams. It explores how civic institutional buildings can be repurposed for public social welfare, preserving community memory through residential sanctuary.
